# AI Omnibus 2026: What Changes for Companies and Law Firms After It Takes Effect

Practical analysis of the AI Omnibus entering into force on July 27, 2026: simplification of obligations for high-risk AI systems, new deadlines, and how it affects businesses and law firms.

## Introduction

**July 27, 2026**, marked a turning point in artificial intelligence regulation in Europe. The ***AI Omnibus***, adopted in June as part of the *Digital Simplification Package*, entered into force with targeted amendments to the *AI Act* that streamline obligations for companies and clarify the regulatory framework.

For law firms, compliance officers, and tech companies, this is not a minor change: it is an **opportunity to recalibrate compliance strategies** and take advantage of the greater flexibility introduced by the European Commission.

In this article, we analyze what changes with the *AI Omnibus*, how it affects high-risk AI systems, and what concrete actions organizations must take in the coming weeks.

---

## 1. What Is the AI Omnibus and Why Now?

The *AI Omnibus* is a package of amendments to the *AI Act* designed to **reduce the regulatory burden** without compromising the protection of fundamental rights. The European Commission recognized that, after the first months of *AI Act* application, certain obligations were excessively burdensome for SMEs, startups, and even large companies in non-critical sectors.

### Context: The AI Act Before the Omnibus

The *AI Act* established a risk-based classification framework:

| Risk Level | Examples | Obligations |
|------------|----------|-------------|
| Unacceptable | Cognitive manipulation, social scoring | Banned since February 2025 |
| High | Healthcare, education, justice, employment | Conformity, registration, audits |
| Limited | Chatbots, deepfakes | Transparency |
| Minimal | Recommenders, spam filters | Voluntary |

The problem: the **high-risk** category absorbed too many compliance resources, especially for companies using AI as a support tool rather than as a critical decision-making system.

---

## 2. Main Changes in the AI Omnibus

### 2.1. Simplification for Non-Critical High-Risk Systems

The *Omnibus* introduces a **sub-classification within high-risk systems**:

- **Critical high risk**: Systems in critical infrastructure, healthcare, transport, and justice. All original obligations remain.
- **Standard high risk**: Systems in education, employment, and non-essential public services. **Reduced obligations** for conformity assessment and documentation.

This means that a company using AI for **CV screening** no longer has to comply with the same regime as a medical diagnosis system.

### 2.2. Exemptions for Internal Use and Development

Companies that develop or use AI **exclusively for internal use** and without impact on third parties are exempt from certain registration and external audit requirements, provided they carry out a **documented internal impact assessment**.

### 2.3. New Transitional Deadlines

The *Omnibus* extends adaptation deadlines for certain systems:

| System | Previous Deadline | Current Deadline (Omnibus) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| GPAI with systemic risk | August 2026 | November 2026 |
| Standard high-risk systems | August 2026 | October 2026 |
| Critical high-risk systems | August 2026 | **No change** |

> ⚠️ **Important**: Critical high-risk systems **do not get an extension**. The August 2026 deadline remains in force for them.

### 2.4. Clarification on Open Source

Open-source AI models are **excluded from registration obligations** as long as they do not pose a systemic risk. This is a win for the European open-source AI ecosystem.

## 3. Impact on Law Firms

### 3.1. New Advisory Opportunities

The *AI Omnibus* generates demand for legal services in three areas:

1. **System reclassification**: Helping clients determine whether their systems move from "high risk" to "standard high risk" or even "limited."
2. **DPIA updates**: Previous impact assessments must be updated to reflect the new sub-classification.
3. **Vendor contracts**: Renegotiating DPAs (*Data Processing Agreements*) and adding clauses about *Omnibus* compliance.

### 3.2. Reduced Burden for Internal Tools

Law firms using AI for:
- Internal contract review
- Case law research
- Document analysis

Fall, in many cases, into the **internal use exemption** category, as long as they do not affect client decisions or third parties.

### 3.3. Risk of Over-Exemption

> "The temptation to reclassify everything as 'internal use' is high, but the AEPD and the European Commission have warned that documented justification will be required."

Law firms must rigorously document why a system qualifies as exempt. Lack of documentation may be considered non-compliance.

## 4. Post-Omnibus Action Checklist

### For Tech Companies

- [ ] **System audit**: Reclassify each system according to the new *Omnibus* typology.
- [ ] **Update DPIAs**: Incorporate the sub-classification and justify the assigned category.
- [ ] **Review contracts**: Ensure providers and clients reflect the new framework.
- [ ] **Train the team**: Train compliance and technical staff on the changes.
- [ ] **Document exemptions**: If an exemption applies, keep a written record of the analysis.

### For Law Firms

- [ ] **Offer AI *health checks*** to clients: quick review of their post-*Omnibus* compliance status.
- [ ] **Update compliance *playbooks*** with the new typology.
- [ ] **Train lawyers** on the difference between critical and standard high risk.
- [ ] **Review contract templates** to include references to the *Omnibus*.

---

## 5. The AI Office and New Governance

The European Commission's *AI Office*, with more than 125 staff and 6 specialized units, has taken a central role in interpreting the *Omnibus*. Its technical guidelines, expected for September 2026, will be key to understanding how to apply the new subcategories in practice.

Companies and law firms should monitor:
- *AI Office* publications on updated codes of practice.
- Decisions of the *European Artificial Intelligence Board* (AI Board).
- Actions by national market authorities (in Spain, the AEPD and the CNMC).

---

## 6. Comparison: Original AI Act vs. AI Omnibus

| Aspect | AI Act (Original) | AI Omnibus (July 2026) |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Classification | 4 risk levels | 4 levels + high-risk sub-classification |
| Standard high risk | Same regime as critical | Reduced obligations |
| Internal use | No distinction | Documented exemptions |
| Open-source GPAI | Mandatory registration | Excluded if not systemic |
| Deadlines | August 2026 for all | Staggered until November 2026 |
| Penalties | Up to €35M or 7% turnover | Maintained, but with proportionality criteria |

---

## 7. Trends and Predictions

### Towards a More Pragmatic Framework

The *AI Omnibus* reflects a clear trend in the EU: **regulate without suffocating**. The Commission has understood that Europe cannot compete in AI if its companies devote more resources to compliance than to innovation.

### Opportunity for the Legal Sector

Law firms that master the *Omnibus* will have a competitive advantage. It is not just about avoiding penalties: it is about **helping clients move faster** within a framework that, although more flexible, remains complex.

### Persistent Regulatory Risk

Despite the simplification, penalties remain: up to **€35 million or 7% of global turnover**. Proportionality in their application will depend on the national authority, introducing legal uncertainty that law firms will need to manage.

---

## Conclusion

The entry into force of the *AI Omnibus* on July 27, 2026, is not the end of the *AI Act*: it is its **first evolution**. For companies and law firms, it represents an opportunity to recalibrate strategies, reduce unnecessary compliance costs, and, above all, position themselves as experts in a framework that will continue to change.

The immediate actions are clear: audit, reclassify, document, and train. Those who do so in the coming weeks will have an advantage over those who wait for authorities to publish guidelines.
