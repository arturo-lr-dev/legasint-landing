# Data Quality in AI: AEPD Guide for GDPR Compliance

Analysis of the AEPD technical note on data quality, accuracy, and minimization in AI systems and its impact on law firms.

## Introduction

On July 21, 2026, the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) published a pioneering technical note analyzing how the principles of **accuracy and minimization under the GDPR** apply to data processing involving artificial intelligence systems. This document, one of the first comprehensive analyses worldwide, establishes an interpretive framework that enables the application of GDPR principles in AI environments without falling into overly restrictive interpretations that could hinder the development of these systems.

For law firms advising companies on data protection and artificial intelligence, understanding this guide is essential to provide precise and up-to-date counsel.

## 1. The Principle of Accuracy in the Context of AI

### Beyond Absolute Truthfulness

The AEPD clarifies a fundamental point: the principle of accuracy set out in **Article 5.1(d) of the GDPR** does not require that data always be fully truthful or completely up-to-date. Instead, it establishes that data must be **adequate for fulfilling the purpose of the processing** in which they are used.

This interpretation represents an evolution from more restrictive approaches that linked accuracy exclusively to the truthfulness of data. The Agency emphasizes that excessive requirements in this regard could conflict with the principle of minimization or generate disproportionate burdens, especially in the development and use of artificial intelligence systems.

> "The principle of accuracy is not understood in absolute terms, but in relation to the purpose of the processing."

### Data Quality in Machine Learning Models

The technical note explains that data quality must be analyzed not only in relation to each individual data point, but especially with respect to the **dataset as a whole** used for developing AI systems. In these systems, the representativeness, absence of bias, or relevance of the data can be decisive for the outcome, even above the individual accuracy of each record.

| Aspect | Relevance in AI |
|--------|-----------------|
| Individual accuracy | Important for decisions about specific individuals |
| Dataset representativeness | Critical for machine learning models |
| Absence of bias | Decisive for avoiding algorithmic discrimination |
| Feature relevance | Fundamental for model suitability |

## 2. Projecting Data Quality Across the Entire Lifecycle

### Beyond Input Data

The AEPD highlights that the data quality requirement is not limited to input data, but must be projected across **the entire processing operation**, including results generated by artificial intelligence systems, such as inferences, predictions, or decisions.

This implies evaluating the suitability of the processing as a whole, taking into account:
- System design
- Operations performed
- Safeguards incorporated

### Variable Level of Accuracy Depending on Impact

The Agency notes that the level of accuracy required may vary depending on the type of processing and its effects on individuals:

- **Aggregated or statistical processing**: May admit greater levels of abstraction or data transformation
- **Decisions about specific individuals**: The principle of accuracy must be applied as extensively as possible

## 3. AI Techniques and GDPR Compatibility

### Synthetic Data and Anonymization

The note addresses the use of common techniques in AI system development:

- **Synthetic data generation**
- **Anonymization**
- **Bias correction**

The AEPD clarifies that these practices can be compatible with the principle of accuracy under the GDPR, even when they involve using values that do not directly reflect individual reality, provided they are justified in relation to the purpose of the processing and do not generate adverse effects on individuals.

### Development vs. Deployment

One of the most highlighted aspects is the distinction between data quality requirements in the **development** phase and the **deployment** phase:

| Phase | Data Quality Requirements |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Development | Focus on representativeness and minimization of biases |
| Deployment | Greater emphasis on accuracy of results for specific individuals |

The Agency emphasizes that both phases must be evaluated differently, taking into account that in the deployment phase, results can produce direct effects on individuals.

## 4. Practical Implications for Law Firms

### Advising Clients

Law firms must consider these points when advising companies that develop or use AI systems:

1. **Define objective data quality requirements** based on the purpose of the processing
2. **Document these requirements** explicitly
3. **Establish data quality metrics**
4. **Conduct continuous monitoring** throughout the processing lifecycle

### Data Governance

The AEPD highlights the importance of implementing **data governance** as a key element for compliance. This includes:

- Data quality policies
- Clear roles and responsibilities
- Control and audit procedures
- Team training

### Non-Personal Data

The Agency reminds us that quality requirements do not apply solely to personal data. **Non-personal data** used in processing can also be decisive for its suitability, especially in AI models where the correlation between non-personal and personal variables can generate unexpected results.

## 5. Safeguards and Limitations of AI

The technical note emphasizes that GDPR compliance requires designing processing operations that adequately manage the **limitations of artificial intelligence systems** through appropriate measures and safeguards:

- **Human review** of automated decisions
- **Explainability mechanisms** for the model
- **Periodic audits** of quality and biases
- **Right to rectification** of inaccurate data
- **Continuous oversight** procedures

## 6. Compliance Checklist for Law Firms

### When Advising on AI Implementation

- [ ] Has the purpose of the processing been defined clearly and specifically?
- [ ] Have objective data quality requirements been established and documented?
- [ ] Has the representativeness of the training dataset been evaluated?
- [ ] Have potential biases in the data been identified and mitigated?
- [ ] Is there a distinction between quality requirements for development and deployment?
- [ ] Have safeguards been implemented for decisions about specific individuals?
- [ ] Is there a human review procedure for results?
- [ ] Have metrics and continuous quality monitoring been established?

## Conclusion

The AEPD's technical note represents a significant advance in understanding how to apply the GDPR in artificial intelligence environments. Its pragmatic approach, which avoids overly restrictive interpretations without sacrificing the protection of individuals, provides a useful framework for both companies and legal advisors.

For law firms, mastering these criteria is essential for providing valuable advice in an area where regulation and technology are constantly evolving. The key is to understand that data quality in AI is not an absolute concept, but must be evaluated in relation to the purpose of the processing and the potential impact on affected individuals.
