# From Idea to App: The 7 Development Phases You Need to Know

Discover the 7 essential phases of software development, from initial idea to launch and beyond. A practical guide for businesses and entrepreneurs.

## Where Does a Software Project Really Begin?

You have an idea. Maybe it's an app to automate internal processes, a platform for customers, or a tool that will solve a specific problem in your industry. Whatever it is: the idea is there, clear in your head.

But now what?

Most software projects fail not because of a lack of good ideas, but because phases are skipped. Companies that start coding without strategy. Startups that invest months in development without validating with users. Teams that launch products nobody needs.

At LegaSint, we've accompanied dozens of companies on this journey. And we've identified **7 phases** that separate successful projects from those that end up in the drawer.

These aren't textbook theories. They're the reality of building software that works, gets used, and generates value.

## Phase 1: Discovery and Problem Definition

### What Most People Get Wrong

Jumping directly to "what technology do we use?" or "how much does it cost?" before understanding what problem is being solved.

### What You Should Do

Before writing a single line of code, before designing a screen, before even choosing a provider:

**Define the problem with surgical precision.**

Not "we want to digitize the company." That means nothing.

Yes: "Our manual invoicing process takes 3 days, generates errors in 12% of cases, and delays collections by an average of 15 days."

### Key Questions in This Phase

1. **What specific process are we improving?** (Be specific, not generic)
2. **Who suffers from it and how much does it cost them?** (In time, money, frustration)
3. **What happens if we do nothing?** (Quantify the cost of inaction)
4. **How is it done today and why is it insufficient?** (Understand the current process)

### Estimated Duration

1-2 weeks. Includes user interviews, analysis of current processes, and problem documentation.

### Deliverable

A **Problem Statement** document that anyone can read and understand what is being solved, why it matters, and for whom.

## Phase 2: Idea Validation

### What Most People Get Wrong

Assuming that because the idea seems good to you, users need it. Building for months without talking to anyone who will actually use it.

### What You Should Do

**Validate before building.**

Validation isn't asking your team "does this seem like a good idea?" It's getting out there (metaphorically) and checking if someone would pay for this, use it, or if it solves a real pain point.

### Quick Validation Methods

- **Discovery interviews:** 5-10 conversations with potential users. Don't sell. Listen.
- **Validation landing page:** A page describing the solution and measuring interest (emails collected, reservations, etc.)
- **Paper prototype:** Draw screens on paper and have users "navigate" simulating the flow
- **Concierge MVP:** Perform the service manually first. Does it work? Do they ask for more?

### The "No" Rule

If you can't find 5 people who say "yes, I'd use this" or "yes, I'd pay for this," your idea needs to pivot. Better to discover it in 2 weeks than in 6 months of development.

### Estimated Duration

2-4 weeks.

### Deliverable

**Documented validation:** evidence that demand exists, summarized interviews, and decision to continue, pivot, or stop.

## Phase 3: Strategy and Scope

### What Most People Get Wrong

Wanting to build everything in the first version. The "kitchen sink syndrome": if I can add this feature, and this other one, and also this one... The result is a project that never ends.

### What You Should Do

**Define the minimum viable scope that solves the main problem.**

This isn't randomly cutting features. It's identifying the 20% of features that solve 80% of the problem.

### The Prioritization Framework

For each proposed feature, ask yourself:

1. **Does it solve the main problem?** (If not, it's nice-to-have, not must-have)
2. **Without this, does the product work?** (If yes, postpone it)
3. **How many users would use it?** (If it's less than 20%, postpone it)
4. **How much does it cost to implement vs the value it provides?** (Negative ROI = no)

### The Difference Between MVP and Complete Product

| | MVP | Complete Product |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Validate the solution with minimum effort | Serve all use cases |
| **Features** | Core only | Core + edge cases + integrations |
| **Users** | Tolerant early adopters | Demanding end users |
| **Time** | 6-12 weeks | 6-12 months |
| **Budget** | €15k-€50k | €100k+ |

### Estimated Duration

1-2 weeks.

### Deliverable

**Product Roadmap** with defined MVP, prioritized features, and plan for subsequent phases.

## Phase 4: UX/UI Design

### What Most People Get Wrong

Treating design as "making it pretty" at the end. Or worse: not doing design and letting developers "improvise as they go."

### What You Should Do

**Design the experience before coding.**

Design isn't aesthetics. It's **how it works**. It's the difference between an app people use daily and one they abandon after the first try.

### Design Stages

1. **Information architecture:** What screens exist? How do you navigate between them?
2. **Wireframes:** Black and white schematics of each screen. No colors, no photos. Just structure.
3. **Interactive prototype:** Connected wireframes to simulate navigation. Have real users test it.
4. **Visual design (UI):** Colors, typography, components. The visual layer that makes it pleasant to use.

### Why This Saves Money

Moving a button in a design costs 30 minutes. Moving it in developed code costs 3 hours. Moving it when it's already in production costs 3 days.

**Designing first is cheaper than fixing later.**

### Estimated Duration

2-4 weeks (for MVP).

### Deliverable

**Final design in Figma/Sketch** with navigable prototype and specifications for developers.

## Phase 5: Development

### What Most People Get Wrong

Waiting to see something functional at the end. Having no visibility into progress. Not testing until "it's finished."

### What You Should Do

**Iterative development with frequent deliveries.**

Modern development isn't a black box from which a finished product emerges 6 months later. It's a transparent process with milestones every 1-2 weeks.

### Recommended Methodology: Agile/Scrum

- **1-2 week sprints** with clear objectives
- **Demo at the end of each sprint** where you see real functionalities
- **Retrospectives** to improve the process
- **Prioritized backlog** that you can review and adjust

### What You Should Demand From Your Provider

- [ ] **Code repository you have access to** from day 1
- [ ] **Testing environments** where you can see progress in real-time
- [ ] **Automated testing** that runs with every change
- [ ] **Technical documentation** that updates during development
- [ ] **Frequent deployments** to staging environment

### Estimated Duration

6-12 weeks (for MVP).

### Deliverable

**Functional application in staging environment**, ready for user testing.

---

## Phase 6: Testing and QA

### What Most People Get Wrong

Testing only the "happy path." Assuming that if it works on the developer's computer, it will work everywhere. Not involving real users until launch.

### What You Should Do

**Test systematically before launching.**

Testing isn't a luxury. It's what separates products that work from those that embarrass your company.

### Essential Testing Types

1. **Functional testing:** Does it do what it should do?
2. **Usability testing:** Do users understand how to use it without a manual?
3. **Compatibility testing:** Does it work on different browsers/devices?
4. **Performance testing:** Does it load in less than 3 seconds?
5. **Security testing:** Are user data protected?
6. **UAT (User Acceptance Testing):** Real users test the product before launch

### The Cost of Not Testing

A bug found in development costs 1x to fix. In testing, 5x. In production, 30x. When it affects customers, the reputational cost is incalculable.

### Estimated Duration

1-2 weeks.

### Deliverable

**QA Report** with resolved bugs, quality metrics, and approval for launch.

---

## Phase 7: Launch and Post-Launch

### What Most People Get Wrong

Treating launch as the end. "That's it, it's over." Not measuring, not iterating, not improving.

### What You Should Do

**Treat launch as the beginning.**

Launch day is when you really start learning. Real users behave differently than you expected. Use cases emerge that you didn't contemplate. Bugs appear that you didn't detect.

### Launch Checklist

- [ ] **Active monitoring:** Is it online? Is it working? Are there errors?
- [ ] **Configured analytics:** Who's using it? How? Where do they drop off?
- [ ] **Support plan:** Who responds if something fails?
- [ ] **User communication:** Do they know it exists? Do they know how to use it?
- [ ] **Rollback plan:** Can we go back if something goes wrong?

### Phase 7.5: Continuous Iteration

Living software requires:

- **Security updates** (monthly)
- **Dependency maintenance** (quarterly)
- **New features** based on user feedback
- **Performance optimization** based on real data
- **Maintenance budget:** 15-20% of development cost annually

### Estimated Duration

Launch: 1 week. Iteration: continuous.

### Deliverable

**Product in production** with monitoring, analytics, and iteration plan.

## The Realistic Timeline: How Long Does All This Take?

| Phase | Duration | Cumulative |
|------|----------|-----------|
| 1. Discovery | 1-2 weeks | 2 weeks |
| 2. Validation | 2-4 weeks | 6 weeks |
| 3. Strategy/Scope | 1-2 weeks | 8 weeks |
| 4. UX/UI Design | 2-4 weeks | 12 weeks |
| 5. Development | 6-12 weeks | 24 weeks |
| 6. Testing | 1-2 weeks | 26 weeks |
| 7. Launch | 1 week | 27 weeks |

**Total for MVP: 5-7 months**

This is for a well-done MVP. Projects promising "your app in 2 weeks" or "MVP in 1 month" are usually cutting corners that later cost double to fix.

## The 3 Signs That Something Is Going Wrong

During any of these phases, watch out for these warning signs:

1. **"We don't need to validate, we already know what they want"** → Arrogance that costs dearly
2. **"We'll add features as we go"** → Scope creep that never ends
3. **"We don't need to test, we're sure it works"** → Technical debt that pays interest

If you hear any of these phrases, stop and review.
