# Custom CRM: Why Salesforce Isn't Always the Answer

Discover when a custom CRM outperforms generic solutions like Salesforce: complex sales flows, specific integrations, and long-term costs.

## The "Market Leader" Trap

Salesforce generated over $34 billion in revenue in 2025. It has 150,000 customers. It appears as a "leader" in every Gartner quadrant. So why are more and more mid-sized and large companies abandoning it or complementing it with custom developments?

The answer isn't that Salesforce is bad. It's that **a generic CRM designed for "every company in the world" rarely fits perfectly into a specific business**. And when it doesn't fit, you pay the price in adaptation hours, workarounds, forced integrations, and —worst of all— lost sales opportunities due to friction in the process.

At LegaSint, we've migrated companies from Salesforce, HubSpot, and Zoho to custom CRMs. We've also integrated these generic CRMs with proprietary systems. The conclusion: **the right decision depends on your complexity, not your budget**.

---

## When a Generic CRM Works (and When It Doesn't)

### ✅ Salesforce/HubSpot Are a Good Choice If:

- You have a **standard sales process**: lead → contact → opportunity → proposal → close.
- Your sales team is **small or medium-sized** (up to 50 sales reps).
- You need to **start quickly** (in weeks, not months).
- Your integrations are with popular tools (Gmail, Outlook, Slack, Zoom).
- Your budget for customization is limited.

### ❌ A Custom CRM Becomes Essential When:

- Your **sales flows are complex** and don't fit the standard linear pipeline.
- You need **deep integrations** with proprietary systems (ERP, production, logistics).
- The cost of Salesforce licenses for 200+ users exceeds custom development within 2-3 years.
- You need **specific automations** that standard workflows don't cover.
- The **user experience** of the CRM directly affects adoption by the sales team.

## 5 Signs You Need a Custom CRM

### 1. You Spend More Time Adapting the CRM Than Using It

If your sales team spends hours each week creating custom fields, configuring workflows that almost work, or exporting data to Excel to "complete what the CRM doesn't do," you're paying for a tool that slows you down.

> **Real case:** A B2B industrial company had a sales process with 12 stages, interdepartmental technical validations, and engineering approvals. In Salesforce, this required 3 custom objects, 14 workflows, and a dashboard no one understood. The sales team used a parallel Excel sheet. The CRM was decorative.

### 2. Integrations Are a Nightmare

Salesforce has AppExchange. HubSpot has native integrations. But when you need to connect your CRM with:

- A **SAP/Oracle ERP** with complex master data
- A **production system** that marks real-time availability
- A **pricing platform** with proprietary business rules
- A **customer portal** where the client sees their order status

..."standard" integrations become 6-month projects with external consultants. And they remain fragile.

### 3. License Costs Grow Disproportionately

| Scenario | Users | Annual Salesforce Cost* | Annual Custom CRM Cost** |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B Startup | 10 | €18,000 | €35,000 (development + hosting) |
| Mid-sized Company | 50 | €90,000 | €45,000 |
| Large Enterprise | 200 | €360,000 | €60,000 |
| Corporation | 500 | €900,000 | €90,000 |

\* Approximate Salesforce Enterprise prices (~€150/user/month)  
\* Amortized over 3 years, including maintenance

**The inflection point is usually between 50 and 100 users.** Beyond that, a custom CRM isn't just more flexible: it's cheaper.

### 4. You Need Speed of Change That SaaS Can't Provide

Generic CRMs release new features quarterly. But you don't control the roadmap. If your competitor launches a new subscription model and you need to adapt your pipeline in 2 weeks, depending on Salesforce's release cycle can be lethal.

With a custom CRM:

- **Changes in days, not months.**
- **A/B experimentation** of sales processes without depending on global configurations.
- **Feature flags** to test new flows with a subset of sales reps.

### 5. User Experience Is Critical for Adoption

Salesforce is powerful. It's also overwhelming. The learning curve is steep and the interface is densely populated with fields that half the teams don't use.

A custom CRM can be designed with **exactly what each role needs to see**:

- The sales rep sees only their opportunities, daily tasks, and alerts.
- The sales director sees dashboards, forecasting, and portfolio risks.
- The pre-sales technician sees pending technical requirements to validate.
- The CEO sees the consolidated pipeline and conversion metrics.

**No noise. No unused tabs. No 3-day training to understand the interface.**

## Custom CRM: How Is It Built?

### Phase 1: Analysis of Current Sales Process (2-3 weeks)

Before writing a single line of code, we map:

- **Real sales flows** (not the org chart ones, the ones people actually do).
- **Friction points** where opportunities are lost.
- **Data shared** with other departments (production, finance, logistics).
- **Necessary integrations** and their technical complexity.
- **Roles and permissions** by user type.

### Phase 2: Architecture Design (1-2 weeks)

We decide:

- **Technology stack:** Node.js/Python + React/Vue + PostgreSQL/MongoDB is standard, but depends on your ecosystem.
- **Data model:** Entities, relationships, calculated fields, historical data.
- **Integration APIs:** Which systems the CRM talks to and how.
- **Scalability:** How many users, what data volume, what expected performance.

### Phase 3: CRM MVP (6-10 weeks)

We build the minimum version that allows:

- Managing the complete sales pipeline.
- Recording customer interactions.
- Generating the basic reports the business needs.
- Integrating with the 1-2 critical systems.

### Phase 4: Iteration and Scaling (ongoing)

With the MVP in production:

- We measure adoption and friction.
- We add automations based on real data.
- We integrate additional systems.
- We optimize performance according to growth.

## The Most Common Mistake: Thinking "Custom" = "Always More Expensive"

The usual prejudice is that a custom CRM is a luxury for large corporations. The reality:

- **For 10 users with standard processes:** Salesforce wins. No discussion.
- **For 100 users with complex processes:** The custom CRM is usually 40-60% cheaper over 3 years.
- **For 500+ users:** Salesforce can cost over one million euros annually. A custom CRM, amortized, is a fraction.

And that's without counting the **opportunity cost**: a CRM that adapts to your business instead of forcing your business to adapt to it.

---

## Hybrid: The Third Way That Works

It's not always black or white. Many successful companies use a hybrid model:

- **Salesforce/HubSpot as base CRM** for lead management, email marketing, and standard reporting.
- **Custom modules** for the specific processes the generic one doesn't cover: pricing, product configuration, post-sales project management.
- **API integration** so both worlds share data in real time.

This approach leverages the best of both worlds: SaaS implementation speed + custom development flexibility where it really matters.

## Checklist: Generic or Custom CRM?

Answer these questions:

- [ ] Do you have more than 50 CRM users?
- [ ] Does your sales process have more than 5 stages with interdepartmental validations?
- [ ] Do you need to integrate with proprietary systems (ERP, production, logistics)?
- [ ] Would annual license costs exceed €50,000?
- [ ] Do you need to change sales processes frequently (quarterly or more)?
- [ ] Is adoption of the current CRM by the sales team low?
- [ ] Do you export data to Excel to "complete" what the CRM doesn't do?

**If you check 3+:** Seriously evaluate a custom CRM or a hybrid approach.
