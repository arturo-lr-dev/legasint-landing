# AI Act: What Happens After the August 2026 Deadline

Guide on the consequences, sanctions, and ongoing obligations after the AI Act compliance deadline for high-risk AI systems in August 2026.

## Introduction

**August 2, 2026** marks a historic turning point for artificial intelligence regulation in Europe. With the compliance deadline for high-risk AI systems under the AI Act finally reached, many companies and law firms are asking: **what now?**

This article analyzes the practical consequences after the deadline: what obligations continue, what sanctions come into play, and how to maintain long-term compliance.

## 1. The Landscape After August 2, 2026

### What Exactly Has Changed?

From this date onward:

- **All high-risk AI systems** must be fully compliant with the Regulation
- **National authorities** have active sanctioning powers
- **Providers and deployers** must maintain records, assessments, and updated documentation
- **The digital single market** requires conformity to market or use these systems in the EU

> ⚠️ **Important:** The deadline is not a finish line, but the **beginning of a continuous compliance regime**.

## 2. Sanctions and Fines: What's at Stake

### AI Act Sanctions Structure

| Type of Infringement | Maximum Sanction | Legal Basis |
|---------------------|------------------|-------------|
| Use of prohibited AI systems | €35M or 7% global turnover | Art. 99.3 |
| Non-compliance with high-risk system obligations | €15M or 3% global turnover | Art. 99.4 |
| Providing incorrect information to authorities | €7.5M or 1% global turnover | Art. 99.5 |
| Non-compliance by SMEs and startups | Same but with some flexibility | Art. 99.5 |

### Who Can Be Sanctioned?

- **Providers:** Companies that develop or market AI systems
- **Deployers:** Organizations that use third-party AI systems
- **Distributors and importers:** Actors in the value chain
- **Authorized representatives:** In the EU for foreign providers

## 3. Ongoing Obligations Post-Deadline

### For High-Risk System Providers

1. **Risk Management System** (Art. 9)
   - Identification and analysis of known and foreseeable risks
   - Estimation and evaluation of risks that may emerge during use
   - Evaluation of other possible risks
   - Testing to manage the most relevant risks

2. **Data and Dataset Governance** (Art. 10)
   - Data governance practices for training, validation, and testing data
   - Review of biases, omissions, or limitations
   - Appropriate mitigation measures

3. **Technical Documentation** (Art. 11)
   - Continuous updating of documentation
   - Demonstration of conformity
   - Availability for national authorities

4. **Record-Keeping and Traceability** (Art. 12)
   - Automatic logging of events
   - Retention throughout the system's lifecycle
   - Ability to trace each individual decision

5. **Transparency and Information Provision** (Art. 13)
   - Clear instructions for use
   - Known limitations and risks
   - Input specifications and intended use context

6. **Human Oversight** (Art. 14)
   - Measures to facilitate output interpretation
   - Ability to override or reverse decisions
   - Adequate training for supervisory staff

### For Deployers (Business Users)

- **Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment** before deployment
- **Notification to workers** about AI system use
- **Operational oversight** during use
- **Log retention** automatically generated
- **Cooperation with authorities** in case of incidents

## 4. What If You Still Don't Comply?

### Urgency Assessment

```markdown
Step 1: Identify if your system is high-risk
- Is it used in critical areas (education, employment, justice, etc.)?
- Does it affect fundamental rights or safety?
- Is it included in Annex III of the AI Act?

Step 2: Determine the current risk level
- Is the system already in production?
- Is it being used in the EU?
- Are personal data involved?

Step 3: Prioritize corrective actions
- Temporary system suspension (if necessary)
- Implementation of mitigation measures
- Request for specialized advice
```

### The Risk of "Quick Compliance"

Many companies are tempted to do **superficial compliance** to avoid immediate sanctions. This is dangerous because:

- Authorities can request documentation at any time
- Systems must remain compliant throughout their entire lifecycle
- Audits may reveal deficiencies months later
- Reputation is at stake with clients and partners

## 6. Expected Trends and Developments

### Short Term (2026-2027)

- **First wave of sanctions:** Significant sanctioning resolutions are expected
- **Initial jurisprudence:** Courts will begin interpreting the AI Act
- **Detailed guidelines:** The European Commission will publish sectoral guidance
- **Codes of conduct:** Development of specific sectoral standards

### Medium Term (2027-2028)

- **Regulation review:** Impact assessment and possible adjustments
- **International harmonization:** Agreements with other jurisdictions (US, UK)
- **Market maturity:** Consolidation of compliance service providers
- **Integration with other regulations:** Convergence with GDPR, DORA, NIS2

## 7. Post-Deadline Survival Checklist

### AI Providers

- [ ] Updated and accessible technical documentation
- [ ] Implemented and documented risk management system
- [ ] Operational and retained functioning logs
- [ ] Conformity assessment conducted (internal or by third party)
- [ ] Registration in the EU database (if applicable)
- [ ] Authorized representative designated (if foreign provider)
- [ ] Incident response plan activated
- [ ] Human oversight team training completed

### Deployers (Users)

- [ ] Fundamental rights impact assessment conducted
- [ ] Internal AI use policies approved
- [ ] Worker notification completed
- [ ] Provider agreements reviewed (DPA, SLA)
- [ ] Human oversight procedures established
- [ ] Usage logs preserved and protected
- [ ] Whistleblowing channel configured
- [ ] Periodic review scheduled
