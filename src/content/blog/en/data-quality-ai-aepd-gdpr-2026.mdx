## Introduction

On **July 21, 2026**, the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) published a groundbreaking technical note: a comprehensive analysis of how the GDPR principles of **accuracy and data minimization** apply to personal data processing involving artificial intelligence systems. This is one of the first works in the world to systematically address this issue, marking a milestone in AI regulation in Europe.

For **law firms** advising technology companies or implementing AI in their own processes, understanding this guide is not optional—it is a strategic necessity.

## 1. The Context: Why Now?

The proliferation of AI systems in the legal sector—from contract analysis tools to due diligence platforms—has raised an urgent question: **how to reconcile the GDPR accuracy principle with the probabilistic nature of AI models?**

The AEPD responds with a pragmatic approach: accuracy should not be understood in absolute terms, but in relation to the **purpose of the processing**. This nuance is crucial to avoid overly restrictive interpretations that could paralyze innovation.

## 2. Key Conclusions from the Technical Note

### 2.1. Relative, Not Absolute Accuracy

The AEPD clarifies that the GDPR accuracy principle **does not require data to be always fully truthful or completely up-to-date**. What is required is that they be adequate to fulfill the purpose of the processing.

> "The level of accuracy required may vary depending on the type of processing and its effects on individuals."

**Practical implication:** A firm using AI for portfolio statistical analysis can accept higher levels of abstraction than if using AI to generate personalized legal recommendations for specific clients.

### 2.2. Dataset Quality vs. Individual Record Quality

One of the most relevant contributions of the guide is the distinction between:

| Level | Focus | Relevance |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| **Individual record** | Accuracy of each data point | High when affecting specific individuals |
| **Global dataset** | Representativeness, absence of bias, relevance | Critical in machine learning models |

The AEPD emphasizes that in machine learning systems, the **representativeness of the dataset** may be more determinative than the individual accuracy of each record.

### 2.3. Differentiated Phases: Development vs. Deployment

The technical note establishes a key distinction between:

- **Development phase:** Greater flexibility in data quality, focused on representativeness
- **Deployment phase:** Greater guarantees needed, especially when results affect specific individuals

**Practical example:** An AI model trained with historical contract data (development phase) can admit some noise. But when that model generates a recommendation for a specific client (deployment phase), the results must be verifiable and justifiable.

## 3. Techniques Compatible with GDPR

The AEPD explicitly validates several common practices in AI development:

### 3.1. Synthetic Data

Synthetic data generation is compatible with the accuracy principle when:
- It is justified by the purpose of the processing
- It does not generate adverse effects on individuals
- It is adequately documented

### 3.2. Anonymization

Anonymization of data for model training is a valid safeguard, provided it is verified to be non-reversible.

### 3.3. Bias Correction

Bias mitigation techniques (such as dataset rebalancing or attribute weighting) are compatible with GDPR, provided they are documented and do not introduce new biases.

## 4. Data Governance: The Role of the DPO

The AEPD highlights the need to implement **data governance** with:

- **Objectively defined quality requirements**, based on the purpose of processing
- **Established quality metrics** and monitored
- **Lifecycle documentation** of the processing
- **Continuous monitoring** in line with the proactive accountability principle

### Checklist for DPOs and Compliance Officers

```markdown
- [ ] Has the required level of accuracy been defined for each AI processing?
- [ ] Is there a distinction between development and deployment phases?
- [ ] Have data improvement techniques (synthetic, anonymization) been documented?
- [ ] Is there human review for decisions affecting individuals?
- [ ] Have dataset quality metrics been established?
- [ ] Are individuals informed about AI use with their data?
```

## 5. Impact on Law Firms

### 5.1. Advising Technology Clients

Law firms must be prepared to:
- Review **Data Processing Agreements** with AI providers
- Evaluate whether **DPIAs** (Data Protection Impact Assessments) adequately reflect data quality risks
- Advise on the **documentation required** to demonstrate compliance to the AEPD

### 5.2. Internal AI Use

If the firm uses AI tools for:
- **Contract analysis:** Anonymize client data before processing
- **Due diligence:** Limit data shared with the tool to the minimum necessary
- **Litigation outcome prediction:** Ensure transparency about model limitations

## 6. Relationship with AI Act and Other Frameworks

The AEPD technical note complements the **AI Act** framework, especially for high-risk systems. The combination of both regulations creates a compliance ecosystem where:

| Framework | Focus | Key Requirement |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| **GDPR** (via AEPD) | Data quality, accuracy, minimization | Documentation and governance |
| **AI Act** | Algorithmic risk, transparency | Conformity and registration |
| **DORA** (financial sector) | Operational resilience | Technology risk management |

## 7. Recommended Next Steps

### For Companies Developing or Using AI

1. **Audit** current datasets according to AEPD criteria
2. **Document** decisions about data quality and techniques used
3. **Review** contracts with AI providers to include data quality clauses
4. **Train** technical and legal teams on these criteria

### For Law Firms

1. **Incorporate** data quality analysis into DPIAs you draft
2. **Develop** contractual clause templates on data governance in AI
3. **Offer** GDPR compliance audit services for AI projects
4. **Stay updated** on upcoming AEPD and AESIA guidelines
