## Introduction

On **August 2, 2026**, the **AI Omnibus** entered into force—the simplification package that amends the EU Artificial Intelligence Regulation. After months of business complaints about excessive regulatory burden, the European Commission has responded with a set of amendments aimed at reducing bureaucracy, clarifying obligations, and accelerating innovation.

For **law firms** and **legal departments**, the AI Omnibus represents both an opportunity and a new challenge: understanding what changes, what stays the same, and how to advise clients in this new landscape.

In this article, we analyze the main modifications, their practical impact, and the actions law firms must take now.

## 1. What is the AI Omnibus?

The AI Omnibus is part of the European Commission's **Digital Simplification Package**. Its amendments to the AI Act seek to:

- **Reduce administrative burden** for providers and users of AI systems
- **Clarify ambiguous concepts** that generated legal uncertainty
- **Flexibilize requirements** for SMEs and startups
- **Accelerate conformity assessment** procedures

### Context: Why Was It Necessary?

Since the AI Act entered into force in August 2024, multiple sectors reported difficulties:

- Disproportionate compliance costs for medium-sized companies
- Lack of clarity in risk classification
- Slow conformity assessment procedures
- Overlap with other regulations (GDPR, DORA, NIS2)

The Commission estimates that the AI Omnibus will **reduce compliance costs by 25%** for SMEs and **15%** for large companies.

## 2. Main Changes in the AI Omnibus

### 2.1 Simplification for Low-Risk Systems

**Before:** Low-risk AI systems had to comply with extensive documentation requirements.

**Now:** A **simplified category** is introduced with requirements proportional to the actual risk:

| Aspect | Original AI Act | AI Omnibus |
|--------|----------------|------------|
| Technical documentation | Full mandatory | Simplified for low risk |
| EU registry | Mandatory for all | Exempt for low risk |
| Conformity assessment | Third party mandatory | Self-declaration allowed |
| Post-market monitoring | Quarterly | Annual |

### 2.2 New Thresholds for High-Risk Systems

The AI Omnibus **raises the thresholds** for classifying a system as high-risk:

- **Impact criterion:** Now requires **demonstrable significant impact**, not potential
- **Clear exclusions:** Decision support systems (non-automated) are excluded
- **Proportional assessment:** Fewer systems require third-party evaluation

**Practical example:** An AI system that **recommends** legal decisions is no longer automatically classified as high-risk; it must be demonstrated that the final decision is **automatic and binding**.

### 2.3 Relief for SMEs and Startups

The AI Omnibus introduces **specific measures** for companies with fewer than 250 employees:

- **Extended regulatory sandbox:** Trial period of 18 months (previously 12)
- **Free advice:** Access to the Digital Innovation Hubs network
- **Compliance templates:** Standard documentation provided by the Commission
- **Fee reduction:** 50% discount on conformity assessments

### 2.4 Harmonization with Other Regulations

One of the biggest advances is the **clarification of interaction** between the AI Act and other regulations:

```markdown
- **GDPR:** The AI Omnibus specifies that registering AI systems in the EU database does not imply automatic GDPR compliance
- **DORA:** Internal AI systems of financial entities are explicitly excluded from the scope of the AI Act if already regulated by DORA
- **NIS2:** Incident notification requirements are harmonized between both regulations
```

### 2.5 New Transition Periods

The AI Omnibus **extends deadlines** for certain obligations:

| Obligation | Original Deadline | New Deadline |
|------------|------------------|--------------|
| High-risk systems | August 2026 | February 2027 |
| GPAI with systemic risk | August 2026 | November 2026 |
| Codes of conduct | February 2026 | May 2026 |
| Impact assessments | August 2026 | December 2026 |

## 3. Impact on Law Firms

### 3.1 New Advisory Services

The AI Omnibus creates clear business opportunities:

**Reclassification services:**
Many clients who had classified their systems as high-risk can now reclassify them. Law firms can offer:
- Reclassification audits
- Justification documentation
- Defense before competent authorities

**SME advisory:**
The new simplified framework requires specialized guidance:
- Access to regulatory sandboxes
- Compliance with standard templates
- Optimization of conformity costs

### 3.2 Persistent Risks

Despite simplification, certain risks **do not disappear**:

- **Sanctions:** Up to **7% of worldwide turnover** for serious infringements
- **Liability:** The AI Liability Directive maintains its schedule
- **Reputational:** Unethical use of AI remains a brand risk

### 3.3 Internal Team Training

The firm's lawyers must update themselves on:

- New classification thresholds
- Simplified procedures
- Interaction with GDPR, DORA, and NIS2
- Opportunities for clients

## 5. Comparison: AI Act vs AI Omnibus

| Element | AI Act (2024) | AI Omnibus (2026) |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Scope** | All AI systems | Simplified for low risk |
| **Documentation** | Extensive and detailed | Proportional to risk |
| **SMEs** | Same rules as large companies | Special regimes |
| **Assessment** | Frequent third party | Self-declaration possible |
| **Deadlines** | Rigid | Extended and flexible |
| **Sanctions** | Up to 7% turnover | Maintained |
| **Harmonization** | Overlap with other rules | Explicit clarification |

## 6. Practical Cases

### Case A: Law Firm Using AI for Contract Review

**Situation:** The firm uses Claude to review client contracts.

**Before AI Omnibus:**
- Classification: High risk (assistance in legal interpretation)
- Obligations: Third-party evaluation, EU registry, quarterly monitoring

**With AI Omnibus:**
- Classification: **Low risk** (final decision is human)
- Obligations: Simplified documentation, self-declaration
- **Estimated savings:** €15,000-25,000 annually in compliance costs

### Case B: Legaltech Startup for Document Automation

**Situation:** Startup with 15 employees that generates contracts with AI.

**Before AI Omnibus:**
- Compliance costs: ~€50,000/year
- Market access: Slow due to assessments

**With AI Omnibus:**
- Extended sandbox to 18 months
- Free documentation templates
- 50% discount on assessments
- **Estimated savings:** 60% in first-year compliance costs

## 7. The Future: What's Next?

The AI Omnibus is not the end of the regulatory journey. The Commission is already working on:

- **Sector-specific guidelines:** For health, finance, transport
- **Codes of conduct:** Developed by the AI Office with industry
- **Impact assessment:** Study on AI Omnibus effectiveness in 2027
- **New legislation:** Possible AI Liability Directive 2.0
