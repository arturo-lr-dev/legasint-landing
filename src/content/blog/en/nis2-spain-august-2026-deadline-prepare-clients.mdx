## Introduction

August 2026 marks a turning point for cybersecurity in Europe. The NIS2 Directive, adopted in 2022, requires EU Member States to complete its transposition by the end of this month. Spain, which already has a robust Coordination and Governance of Cybersecurity Law, is in the final stretch of this process. For law firms, this is not just regulatory news—it is a business opportunity and an advisory obligation.

In this article, we analyze the current status of transposition, the specific obligations for Spanish companies, and how law firms can position themselves as strategic partners in this process.

## 1. The Context: What Is NIS2 and Why Does It Matter Now?

The NIS2 Directive (2022/2555) expands and strengthens the EU's original cybersecurity framework. Its main objectives are:

- **Expand scope**: from operators of essential services (OES) to a much broader universe of entities, including sectors such as food, manufacturing, research, and waste management.
- **Harmonize requirements**: establish a common minimum level of cybersecurity across the EU.
- **Strengthen sanctions**: include criminal liability for executives in serious cases of non-compliance.
- **Require 24-hour notification**: for significant incidents.

The transposition deadline expired on **17 October 2024**, but many Member States, including Spain, have requested extensions or are finalizing the adaptation of their legal frameworks. The key date for operational compliance is **August 2026**.

## 2. Status of Transposition in Spain

Spain already has **Law 8/2024, of 11 June**, on Coordination and Governance of Cybersecurity. This law has laid the groundwork, but full NIS2 transposition requires:

- **Regulatory development**: the Government must approve provisions that specify technical and organizational requirements.
- **Designation of authorities**: CCN-CERT and INCIBE are strengthening their capabilities as national cybersecurity authorities.
- **Update of the National Security Scheme (ENS)**: aligning it with the most demanding European standards.

## 3. Key Obligations for Companies

### 3.1. Cybersecurity Risk Management

Companies must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures, including:

- Information security policies
- Asset management and system inventory
- Access control and identity management
- Encryption of data in transit and at rest
- Business continuity and disaster recovery plans
- Periodic audits and testing

### 3.2. Incident Notification

- **24 hours**: initial notice to the competent authority
- **72 hours**: complete report with impact assessment
- **1 month**: final report with lessons learned

### 3.3. Management Liability

NIS2 introduces **personal liability** for executives in cases of serious non-compliance. This includes:

- Fines of up to €10 million or 2% of total worldwide turnover
- Administrative sanctions against executives
- In extreme cases, criminal liability
