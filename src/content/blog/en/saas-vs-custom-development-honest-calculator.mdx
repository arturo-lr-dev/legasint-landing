## The Question That Paralyzes Every CEO

> *"Should we buy Salesforce/HubSpot/Monday or build our own CRM?"*

It's the question we've heard hundreds of times. And the usual answer —"it depends"— helps no one.

In this article, we're doing something different: **putting numbers on the table**. With real cases, a decision framework, and the calculator we use with our clients to decide between SaaS and custom development.

There's no single right answer. But there is a right answer *for your case*.

---

## The Most Common Mistake: Comparing Apples to Oranges

Most companies compare:
- **SaaS:** €50/user/month → "cheap"
- **Custom:** €40,000 upfront → "expensive"

And they choose SaaS because the number looks smaller. **Fatal error.**

What they don't see:
- SaaS prices increase 15-20% every year
- You need 3 different SaaS tools that don't talk to each other
- You pay for 200 features when you use 20
- Your team loses 10 hours/week on workarounds
- When you scale, SaaS costs explode

**The honest comparison isn't price vs price. It's TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) over 3-5 years.**

## Real Case #1: The Law Firm That "Saved" Money with SaaS

**Situation:** 15-lawyer firm. Needed case management, billing, and CRM.

**Option A - SaaS stack:**
- CRM (HubSpot): €1,200/year
- Case management (Clio): €3,600/year
- Billing (Facturama): €600/year
- Integrations (Zapier/Make): €1,200/year
- **Year 1 total: €6,600**

**Option B - Custom development:**
- Integrated platform: €35,000
- Maintenance: €4,000/year
- **Year 1 total: €39,000**

**What did they choose?** SaaS. Obvious, right?

### What Happened Next

**Year 2:**
- HubSpot price increase: +€400
- Needed a custom field that Clio didn't have → manual workaround
- Wanted cross-reports (CRM + cases + billing) → impossible without exporting to Excel
- Hired someone for 8 hours/week just to consolidate data: €8,000/year
- **Year 2 total: €15,000+**

**Year 3:**
- Stack grew to 5 tools
- The "Excel guru" left → 3-week operational crisis
- Tried migrating data to a new SaaS → €5,000 in consulting + 2 months of chaos
- **Year 3 total: €22,000+**

**3-year TCO: €43,600+** (and counting)

**Custom would have cost them €43,000** with a platform that does *exactly* what they need, integrated, scalable, and without third-party dependency.

**The SaaS "savings" became a hidden cost of €20,000+ and 3 years of friction.**

## The Calculator: When SaaS and When Custom?

Use this framework. Answer honestly:

### 1. Is Your Process Standard or Specific?

| SaaS | Custom |
|------|--------|
| Email marketing (Mailchimp) | Legal approval flow with 5 roles and conditional rules |
| Basic accounting | Billing with complex business logic |
| Simple task management | Coordinating 3 departments with SLAs |

**Rule:** If 80% of your needs are standard → SaaS. If 50%+ is specific → Custom.

### 2. How Many Users Will You Have in 3 Years?

| Users | Recommendation |
|-------|----------------|
| 1-5 | SaaS almost always |
| 5-20 | Evaluate case by case |
| 20-100 | Custom starts to pay off |
| 100+ | Custom is usually cheaper at 2-3 years |

**Rule:** SaaS scales linearly (€/user). Custom is fixed + maintenance.

### 3. Do You Need Complex Integrations?

If you need 4+ systems to talk in real-time with business logic between them, SaaS will force you to use Zapier/Make with workarounds. **That's not integration, that's patching.**

**Rule:** >3 critical integrations → consider Custom.

### 4. Are Data Your Competitive Advantage?

If your value lies in *how you use data* (predictive analytics, intelligent automation, mass personalization), a generic SaaS limits you. **Your IP should be in your infrastructure, not a third party's.**

**Rule:** Data = competitive advantage → Custom.

### 5. How Fast Do You Need to Change?

SaaS companies have their own roadmap. If you need a feature that doesn't exist, you wait 6-12 months (if it ever comes). With custom, you set the priorities.

**Rule:** Innovation speed is critical → Custom.

## The Break-Even Point

Here's the formula we use:

```
Break-even (months) = Custom development cost / (Monthly SaaS cost - Monthly custom cost)
```

**Example:**
- Custom: €40,000 + €400/month maintenance
- Equivalent SaaS: €2,000/month for 20 users

```
Break-even = 40,000 / (2,000 - 400) = 25 months (~2 years)
```

**After 2 years, custom is cheaper.** And you have a platform that does exactly what you need.

## When to Definitely Choose SaaS

Not everything should be custom. There are cases where SaaS is the obvious answer:

1. **You need something today, not in 3 months**
2. **Your process is 100% standard** (email, basic accounting, calendar)
3. **You have &lt;5 users and limited budget**
4. **You want to test before investing** (process MVP)
5. **You don't have a technical team or trusted partner**

---

## When to Definitely Choose Custom

1. **Your process is your competitive advantage** (you can't use the same as your competition)
2. **You need to integrate 4+ critical systems**
3. **You have >20 users and planned growth**
4. **Data is sensitive or strategic** (health, legal, financial)
5. **Existing SaaS covers 40% of what you need**
6. **You've spent 2 years "adapting" to SaaS limitations**

## The Hybrid: The Option Nobody Tells You About

There's a third way that works very well: **SaaS for standard stuff + custom for specific stuff.**

**Example:**
- Email: Mailchimp (SaaS)
- Accounting: Holded (SaaS)
- Case management: **Custom** (your IP, your flow, your data)
- Integration: API between custom and SaaS

**Advantage:** You get the best of both worlds. Moderate investment. Scalability. Without reinventing the wheel.

## How to Not Regret Your Decision

### If You Choose SaaS:
- Evaluate TCO over 3 years, not the monthly price
- Make sure you can export your data (avoid vendor lock-in)
- Plan for what happens if prices go up 30%
- Document workarounds (they'll cost more than you think)

### If You Choose Custom:
- Choose a partner with experience, not the cheapest
- Start with MVP, not everything at once
- Demand clean, documented, tested code
- Budget maintenance from day 1
- Make sure you can scale without rebuilding

---

## Conclusion: The Honest Calculator

There's no single answer. But there is an *honest* answer:

> **Choose SaaS when your process is standard, your users are few, and you need speed.**
>
> **Choose custom when your process is your advantage, your data is strategic, and growth is inevitable.**

And if you're in the gray zone —which is most likely— run the numbers. Calculate TCO over 3 years. Quantify the cost of workarounds. And decide with data, not with month 1's price.

**Technology is an investment, not an expense. The question isn't how much it costs, but how much it saves you and how much it makes you grow.**
